# The Mechanism

The Mechanism is a research cluster built from found infrastructure: an existing desktop and a
disused Raspberry Pi cluster, with a few pieces added where those ran out. It began as an exercise
in critical technical practice. The aim was to learn about the affordances and constraints of AI for humanities and social science research by engaging in system design and maintenance myself, not paying for online services where I would be one of many paying customers, or renting cloud infrastructure.

The result is unlikely to be implemented by anyone else. I made few concessions to simplicity or future users: it is a radically bespoke system, designed and configured to support my technical and scholarly practices. Some of the design principles and architecture might be useful to people designing AI enabled research platforms, but that would be incidental to my purpose. I am only keeping The Mechanism in service because it has proven to be surprisingly useful for my research, exceeding my expectations for what is closer to a Heath Robinson contraption than a refined computing system. I use The Mechanism on a daily basis, as the primary research tool for my next monograph, *Infrastructural Inflections: Critical Technical Practice and the Architecture of Knowledge*, and for ongoing work related to my current monograph, *Digital Modernity: Why We Need to Think Historically About the Digital Age* (2026). The system is a working research system first, and an experimental research platform second.

This site documents the system at a technical level: what it is, how it works, what it costs, and what
building it has taught me.
